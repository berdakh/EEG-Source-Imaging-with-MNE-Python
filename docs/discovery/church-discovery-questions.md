# Church Pilot: Discovery Interview Guide

## How to run it

- Book 60 minutes with the office administrator. Book a separate 20 minutes with the pastor; they see different problems.
- Sit beside them at their desk, not across a table. Ask them to show, not tell.
- Opening line: "I'm building a free tool that runs entirely on your own computer, nothing goes to the internet. I want to find the one task that eats your week and make it faster. I'm not here to sell anything."
- Do not mention AI features. Ask about their work. Write down verbs.
- Ask for samples of everything they show you (bulletin, newsletter, letters). Real documents beat descriptions.

## 1. People and roles

1. Who works in the office, paid or volunteer, and how many hours a week each?
2. Who is the most computer-comfortable person here? Who is the least?
3. If you were out sick for two weeks, what would fall apart first?

## 2. The weekly rhythm

4. Walk me through your week from Monday to Sunday. What has to be done by when?
5. What do you do every single week that feels like the same thing over again?
6. What do you do every month? Every quarter? At year end?
7. What task do you put off the longest?
8. Where do you spend the most time staring at a blank page?

## 3. Communications

9. Show me the bulletin. How does it get made, start to finish, and how long does it take?
10. Where do announcements come from? Email, texts, paper notes, hallway conversations?
11. Show me the last newsletter or weekly email. What tool sends it? Mailchimp, Constant Contact, Planning Center, Gmail?
12. Who posts on social media and the website? How long does that take?
13. How often do you write the same announcement three different ways for three channels?
14. How do you handle a visitor who fills out a card or form? What happens next?

## 4. Sermons and teaching

15. Are services recorded? Audio, video, or both? Where do the files go?
16. Does anyone turn sermons into notes, summaries, small group guides, or blog posts? Would you if it took five minutes?
17. Does the pastor have a sermon archive they'd want to search, like "when did I preach on forgiveness"?
18. Does the congregation include people who need translation or captions?

## 5. Members, visitors, and records

19. What software holds the member directory? Planning Center, Breeze, ChurchTrac, a spreadsheet, a paper book?
20. How do you look someone up? How do you update an address or a phone number?
21. What reports do you pull from it, and how often?
22. How do you track attendance? Children's check-in?
23. What information about members do you consider sensitive? Who can see it today?

## 6. Giving and finance

24. Who handles giving records? What software? QuickBooks, Tithe.ly, Planning Center Giving, a ledger?
25. How are year-end giving statements produced? How long does that take?
26. How do you handle a benevolence request from someone in need? Is there a form, a letter, a process?
27. What goes to the board or elders each month, and who assembles it?

## 7. Volunteers, events, and scheduling

28. How do you schedule volunteers and ushers and nursery workers? What tool?
29. What happens when someone cancels at the last minute?
30. How many events a month need flyers, emails, sign-ups, and reminders? Who makes those?
31. How are room bookings and the building calendar managed?

## 8. Pastoral care and confidentiality

32. How are pastoral visits, hospital visits, and prayer requests tracked?
33. Does the pastor keep counseling or meeting notes? Where?
34. Is there anything you would never type into a website or an online tool? Why?
35. Has anyone here ever used ChatGPT or similar for church work? For what? Any worries?

## 9. Hardware, IT, and ownership

36. Can I see the computers? Note make, model, age, RAM, Windows or Mac.
37. Is there a shared office computer or does everyone use their own laptop?
38. Who fixes things when the Wi-Fi or printer breaks? A volunteer, a company, nobody?
39. Is there a church email domain and a shared drive? Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, Dropbox?
40. Who would need to say yes for a new tool to be installed on an office computer?

## 10. Pain, priority, and the pilot

41. If I could give you back three hours a week, which task would you want gone?
42. If one task could never have a mistake in it, which one is that?
43. What have you tried before to save time that did not stick? Why did it fail?
44. Would you be willing to try a tool for four weeks and tell me honestly what is wrong with it?
45. Who else here should I talk to?

## What to walk out with

- Sample files: last 3 bulletins, last 3 newsletters, a giving statement with names removed, a volunteer schedule, one event flyer, a board report.
- Exact names of every software product mentioned.
- Photo or notes of the main office computer's specs.
- One sentence from them, in their own words, describing the worst task. That sentence is your first feature.
- A date for a follow-up visit.

## Listen for these

- A task they describe with a sigh. That is the demo.
- Anything that starts with "I retype" or "I copy from".
- Anything where the same content goes to three places.
- "Only Mary knows how to do that." Key-person risk is a feature opportunity.
- Statements about privacy or distrust of online tools. Those are your pitch, in their words.
- Old or weak hardware. It decides whether the pilot runs on their machine or on a small box you bring.
